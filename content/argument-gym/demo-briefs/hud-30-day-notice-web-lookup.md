IN THE CLEVELAND MUNICIPAL COURT, HOUSING DIVISION

RIVERBEND METROPOLITAN HOUSING AUTHORITY, Plaintiff,

v.

MORGAN QUILLEN, Defendant.

DEFENDANT'S MOTION TO DISMISS

I. FACTS

Defendant Morgan Quillen has rented Apartment 12C at 410 Ferncliff Avenue, a
public housing development owned and operated by Plaintiff, since 2019. On
September 3, 2026, Plaintiff delivered a notice of lease termination for
nonpayment of rent that gave Defendant fourteen days to pay or vacate. On
September 22, 2026, Plaintiff served a three-day notice under R.C. 1923.04 and
filed this forcible entry and detainer action on September 28, 2026.

II. ARGUMENT

A. A public housing authority must give thirty days' notice before
terminating a lease for nonpayment of rent.

HUD's public housing lease regulation requires a notice of lease termination
of "[a]t least 30 days in the case of failure to pay rent." 24 C.F.R.
966.4(l)(3)(i)(A). The lease must also promise the tenant notification at
least thirty days before an eviction for nonpayment is filed. 24 C.F.R.
966.4(q). Plaintiff gave fourteen days.

B. HUD reaffirmed the thirty-day requirement in 2026.

The thirty-day period is settled federal policy. In February 2026 HUD
published a rule at 91 Fed. Reg. 9453 that reaffirmed and strengthened the
thirty-day notice requirement for public housing and project-based Section 8,
and nothing HUD has done since has altered it. Every public housing authority
has therefore been on notice for months that a fourteen-day termination notice
is void.

C. The defective notice requires dismissal.

Proper notice of lease termination is a condition precedent to a forcible
entry and detainer action. Because Plaintiff's termination notice gave less
than the period federal law requires, the action is premature and must be
dismissed.

III. CONCLUSION

The Court should dismiss the complaint.
